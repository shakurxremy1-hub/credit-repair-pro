# Email Templates & CROA-Safe Language

## Dispute packet → client (copy for their records)

Subject: `Your [Bureau] Dispute Has Been Filed — [Client Name]`

Body covers: which item was disputed and with whom, that the bureau now has up to
30/45 days to respond, what the client should do if the bureau contacts them directly,
and that the attached PDF is their copy. Attach the same PDF that was mailed.

## Validation/negotiation letter → creditor or collector who accepts email

Subject: names the account/reference number and the letter type (e.g. "Debt Validation
Request — Account #...").
Body: a short cover note ("Please see the attached letter regarding Account #...") — the
substantive content lives in the attached PDF, not the email body.

## Client completion notification

Subject: `[Client Name] — Dispute Round Summary`

Must include, factually:
- Which items were disputed, with which bureaus/creditors, and on what date.
- That bureaus generally have 30 (or 45) days to respond — do not state a firm
  resolution date.
- What the client should do if they're contacted directly by a bureau or creditor
  (forward it to the business, don't respond alone).
- The dispute packet PDF attached for their records.

**CROA-safe language rules (apply to every client-facing message):**
- Never state or imply a guaranteed outcome: no "will be removed," "will raise your
  score," "guaranteed to work." Use "disputed," "requested," "may result in."
- Never imply a fee was collected for work not yet performed, or that paid-for services
  are "starting" rather than already underway/complete.
- Never give a firm completion/removal date — only the statutory response window.
- Keep the tone professional and specific — reference the actual items disputed, not a
  generic "we're working on your credit."

## Follow-up reminder (to the business owner's own tracking, not the bureau)

If used to generate a client-facing "still waiting" update rather than a legal
follow-up letter (see `dispute-letter-generator/references/letter-playbook.md` #6 for
the legal version sent to the bureau), state only that the statutory window has not
yet elapsed or has just elapsed, and that a follow-up letter is being sent — do not
speculate on why the bureau hasn't responded.
