## Mail vs. email vs. online portal

- All three bureaus accept disputes through their own online portals and by mail. None
  of them publish a general-purpose dispute email inbox — do not email a dispute
  directly to a bureau unless the business has a confirmed, bureau-issued intake address
  for its specific use case (e.g. an existing furnisher/data-provider relationship).
- **Default to mail.** A dispute mailed via USPS certified mail with return receipt
  creates a timestamped, provable record of when the 30-day FCRA investigation clock
  started (15 U.S.C. §1681i). This is the standard, defensible method and the one this
  skill's PDFs are formatted for (print, sign, mail certified).
- The `client-communications` skill's email sending is for correspondence that does
  accept email as a matter of course: the client themselves, and creditors/collection
  agencies/furnishers who have given an email address for correspondence. It is not a
  substitute for mailing the bureau dispute itself.

## Response timeline

- Bureaus generally have **30 days** (45 in some cases, e.g. the consumer submitted
  additional information during the investigation) from receipt to investigate and
  respond under FCRA §611.
- If a dispute goes unanswered past the deadline, a follow-up letter citing the missed
  statutory deadline and demanding resolution is appropriate — see
  `../letter-playbook.md` in `client-communications` for templates.
